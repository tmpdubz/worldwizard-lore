# Aurelians

**Alignment**: Lawful Neutral  
**Parent civilization**: [[The Folk]]  
**Homeland**: [[Northwestern Continent]]  
**Government**: Imperial — single Emperor at the apex of a vast bureaucratic apparatus

## Overview

A militant, ordered, disciplined faction that broke away from the gentle Folk. The Folk banished them — *"this is not our time."* The Aurelians have spent every generation since proving them wrong.

From their resettlement in the south of the [[Northwestern Continent]], the Aurelians expanded until their empire encompassed most of the landmass. What began as a banished splinter faction is now the dominant political reality of the continent.

## The Ideology of Order

The Aurelians are true believers. They do not think of themselves as conquerors — they think of themselves as administrators of a better world. In their view, life before the Empire was chaos: arbitrary rule, inefficient farming, no roads, no courts, no consistent law. The Empire fixed that. They are genuinely baffled when people resist, and genuinely convinced that resistance is a temporary condition that education and time will correct.

This sincerity makes them more dangerous than cynical empires, and occasionally more tolerable to live under.

## Government and Bureaucracy

The Emperor sits at the top of a pyramid of administrators, prefects, census-takers, tax collectors, road surveyors, legal clerks, and regional governors. Everything is documented. Everyone has a record. The law is written down, consistently applied, and available to any citizen — which is both the Empire's greatest achievement and its most grinding feature.

Bureaucracy is not a side effect of Aurelian governance. It is the point. Order requires record-keeping. Record-keeping requires clerks. Clerks require supervision. The machine feeds itself.

Daily life under the Empire involves permits, registration, taxation, and an awareness that someone, somewhere, has written your name in a ledger.

## Military and Expansion

The Aurelian military is disciplined, well-supplied, and moves along roads the Empire builds ahead of it. Expansion is methodical — new territory is surveyed, assessed, and administered before the next advance begins. They do not overextend. They consolidate.

Conquered peoples are not massacred or enslaved. They are registered.

## Citizenship

The Empire operates on a tiered system. Conquered peoples enter at the bottom — subject to Aurelian law, required to pay taxes, ineligible for most offices — but the path to full citizenship is real and available. Comply, contribute, learn the language, raise children in the Imperial tradition. Within a generation or two, a family can be fully Aurelian.

This is both genuinely generous and quietly effective at erasing whatever came before.

## Magic and Technology

The Aurelians are more magically and technologically advanced than any other civilization on the [[Northwestern Continent]]. This advancement is inseparable from their governance — magic is not a rare gift among them, it is infrastructure. Enchanted ledgers that update themselves. Scrying networks for communication between prefects. Wards that flag unauthorized movement through imperial checkpoints. Magical engineering that makes their roads, aqueducts, and fortifications faster to build and harder to destroy.

The Empire did not develop this magic for war. It developed it for administration. The military applications were a natural consequence.

This creates a meaningful gap between the Empire and the peoples it absorbs — Aurelian magic-tech is genuinely useful, and access to it is one of the real incentives for pursuing citizenship.

## Origins

The Aurelians are descended from [[The Folk]] — basic human farmers, famously unbothered. The Aurelians carry that agricultural pragmatism in their bones, expressed entirely differently. Where the Folk trusted that things would work out, the Aurelians decided to make sure of it. Their entire civilization is the Folk's contentment turned into anxiety, systematized into empire.
